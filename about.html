Six years on the unglamorous part of software

I've spent six years building production infrastructure for banks that audit everything — identity, evals, and environments where the answer to "what happens when it's wrong" has to survive an audit. That's been my entire professional background: distributed, stateful systems acting on real infrastructure, expensive when wrong.

Finding my way to agents

Most of what gets written about AI agents is about capability. Almost none of it is about the question an operator actually asks before switching one on. That question is the one I've been answering for six years, so I started building: an LLM test report generator shipped into H2O.ai's CI, seventeen-plus Claude Code skills that made our QA lifecycle executable in plain English, and Gauge — deployed and public — where the lesson showed up everywhere: take the decision away from the model, let it write the explanation, and the system becomes something an operator can trust.

These days

Currently a DevOps Engineer at H2O.ai working on QA & certification for Fortune 500 banking clients, from India on US Pacific hours. Looking to bring that same infrastructure discipline to a founding AI engineering role.
